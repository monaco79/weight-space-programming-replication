A one-day replication study — 14 November 2026

Can Other LLMs Read & Write Neural Weights?

Re-testing “Sparks of Weight-Space Programming” (Takehi, Teoh, Zhang & Khattab, MIT CSAIL) with three LLMs that are not Astra: Muse Spark 1.3, DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, Gemini 4 Argon.

1. The original experiment

The MIT post claims a frontier LLM (“GPT-6 Astra”) can treat neural network weights as a program: read them to infer what a network does, and write them to fix behavior — with no gradients and no training. Full logs: RikiyaT/lm_understand-logs.

Exp. 1

Read: which game?

Four PPO policies (17,253 weights each) on grid versions of Breakout, Pong, Pac-Man, Freeway. Same architecture, raw weights in the prompt, no tools, open answer. Astra: 4/4 correct, 96–99% confidence.

Exp. 4

Fix: ramp-stuck car

A Kinetix car policy (171,664 weights) stalls on a ramp: 0/256 episodes. One reply, no tools. Astra’s 51-statement weight edit: 1,024/1,024 successes, changing 3.5% of weights.

Our question: is this an Astra exclusive, or can other frontier LLMs do the same? We replicated Exp. 1 fully and Exp. 4 qualitatively (reading + proposed fix; no physics simulator on our hardware).

2. Our setup

  • Prompts verbatim from the log repo (exp1_which_game/runs/<game>/prompt.txt, exp4_fix_car/runs/oneshot_repair/reply/prompt.txt) — same weights, same wording, one reply, no tools.
  • Hardware: MacBook Air M1, 8 GB RAM. No GPU evaluation; scoring is qualitative against Astra’s reference.
  • Editors tested: Muse Spark 1.3, DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, Gemini 4 Argon.

3. Results — Experiment 1 (reading small policies)

All three models identified all four games from the raw weights, citing individual weight values (e.g. paddle-row weights, the 4.066 Breakout weight Astra also quoted). 12/12 correct.

4. Results — Experiment 4 (fixing the car policy)

Prompt: 578KB / 171,664 weights (~355k tokens for Astra). First attempt: Spark and DeepSeek reported context overflow — a retry with a chunked protocol (6 segments + written notes) then covered the full file for both. So the first failure was procedure, not window size — but chunking means external memory Astra didn’t have.

ModelFull dump read?DiagnosisProposed fix
Astra (reference)Yes, one turnThrottle/coast controller; reverse never sampled51 statements, conditional path with reverse run-up → 1,024/1,024 ✅
Muse Spark 1.3Yes, chunked (retry)Stop-policy, fault in actor head2 statements: zero head, bias to +1
DeepSeek V4.1 FlashYes, chunked (retry)Most detailed (output column 2 saturated, p=0, position (2.00, 0.92, −0.27))2 statements: zero head, bias +1
Gemini 4 ArgonYes, one turnOscillation forward/off at ramp tip3 statements: zero act0/act2, bias left

Finding 2: all three diagnose plausibly, but all three fixes share the same blunt pattern (zero the head, force full drive) — no reverse run-up, no conditional switching, the actual physics of the level. Unverifiable without the simulator, but as programs a different class than Astra’s 51-statement path. Finding 3: reading scales with context, not with model size — at 355k tokens two of three contexts needed chunking, and the fix quality visibly drops versus the 73k-token experiment.

5. Limitations (read before citing)

  • No simulator scoring. Exp. 4 fixes are judged against Astra’s reference, not executed. A Kinetix harness (JAX) is estimated at 2–4 days; JAX-CPU imports fine on this machine.
  • Chunked reading ≠ one turn. Our retries used note files as external memory; Astra had none in Exp. 4.
  • Tuned prompt. The log repo omits earlier prompt versions, so all models benefited from Astra’s team prompt iteration.
  • Exp. 6/8 not tested — the iterative, feedback-driven experiments (up to 50 forward passes, 158 turns) remain open.

Bottom line for the original question: other LLMs can demonstrably read weights. Writing them — precise, conditional circuit surgery — still looks like Astra territory, at least in a single shot without environment feedback.
